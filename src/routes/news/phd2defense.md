On Monday, November 30, a doctoral dissertation defense will take place at the Faculty of Icelandic and Comparative Cultural Studies at the University of Iceland. On this occasion, Nicola Privato will defend his doctoral dissertation in Cultural Studies, entitled Hauntography: Algorithmic Presence and the Out of Joint in Music AI.

The event will take place in the Ceremonial Hall (Hátíðasalur) in the University of Iceland’s Main Building and will begin at 1:00 p.m.

The dissertation’s opponents will be Caroline Bassett, Professor of Digital Humanities at the University of Cambridge, and Jonathan Impett, Research Director of the Orpheus Institute in Ghent.

The dissertation was completed under the supervision of Þórhallur Magnússon, Professor at the University of Iceland, and Einar Torfi Einarsson, Professor at the Iceland University of the Arts.

The doctoral committee also included Franziska Schroeder, Professor at Queen’s University Belfast, and Jón Ólafsson, Professor at the University of Iceland.

Ásgrímur Angantýsson, Dean of the Faculty of Icelandic and Comparative Cultural Studies, will chair the ceremony.

## About the research

Artificial intelligence (AI) technologies are increasingly making their way into creative domains. Within AI systems, deep learning functions like a black box, concealing the processes behind its outputs. The field of Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) has emerged in response to this opacity.

But what is there to explain when AI enters the creative process?

This project takes a critical stance toward the assumptions underlying XAI when applied to the arts. Instead, it explores the aesthetics and discourse that emerge from AI’s inherent opacity, as well as the epistemological possibilities this opacity offers.

Through instrument design, artistic practice, and user research, I demonstrate how artists draw on the unknown qualities of AI models as a source of poetic meaning. Their reflections often converge in artistic discussions of algorithmic presence.

Building on these findings, I develop hauntography, a theoretical framework based on hauntology theory that reconceptualizes AI as a spectral construct. This framework is applied to interviews with artists, offering insights into AI musical interfaces, AI mediation, and ongoing debates surrounding AI agency.

## About the doctoral candidate

Nicola Privato holds a BA in Modern Languages and Literature from the University of Padua (Università degli Studi di Padova, UNIPD), a BA in Jazz Music from the Giuseppe Tartini Conservatory in Trieste, and an MA in Electronic Music Composition from the Cesare Pollini Conservatory in Padua. Nicola lives in Reykjavík and teaches at the Iceland University of the Arts. The doctoral research was conducted at the Intelligent Instruments Lab at the University of Iceland.

# Um rannsóknina

Gervigreindartækni haslar sér í auknum mæli völl innan skapandi greina. Í gervigreindarkerfum virkar djúpnám eins og svartir kassar sem fela ferlið að baki úttökunum. Fræðasviðið útskýranleg gervigreind (XAI) hefur sprottið fram sem viðbragð við þessu. En hvað á að útskýra þegar gervigreind kemur inn í skapandi ferli? Þetta verkefni tekur gagnrýna afstöðu til forsendna XAI þegar beitt er á listgreinar og kannar þess í stað þá fagurfræði og orðræðu sem spretta af innbyggðu ógagnsæi gervigreindar, ásamt þekkingarfræðilegum möguleikum þess. Með hönnun hljóðfæra, listrænni iðkun og notendarannsóknum sýni ég hvernig listamenn nýta hið óþekkta í gervigreindarlíkönum sem uppsprettu skáldlegrar merkingar, en hugleiðingar þeirra koma oft saman í listrænni umfjöllun um reikniritslega nærveru. Út frá þessum niðurstöðum þróa ég vofuritun (e. hauntography), fræðilegan ramma byggðan á vofufræði (e. hauntology) sem endurskilgreinir gervigreind sem vofukennda smíð. Rammanum er beitt á viðtöl við listamenn og veitir innsýn í tónlistarlegt viðmót gervigreindar, miðlun gervigreindar og yfirstandandi umræðu um erindreka gervigreindar.
